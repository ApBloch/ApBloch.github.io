MCU Selection

Pick: ESP32-S3-WROOM, using Espressif's native ESP-IDF (not Arduino)

Why ESP32-S3 over STM32

  • Simpler layout/PCB design overall: not just the antenna — the whole board is simpler to design and route with an ESP32 module.
  • Antenna sold attached to the chip: the WROOM module integrates MCU, BLE radio, and a pre-matched antenna into one solderable component. No separate antenna sourcing or matching-network design.
  • Native USB: ESP32-S3 has built-in USB OTG — no external USB-to-serial chip needed on the board, simpler than STM32's usual USB setup.
  • Chip availability: easy to actually get — a real practical concern after the STM32WB tooling headaches.
  • Already own the hardware: dev boards for prototyping, plus a reel of 10 ESP32-S3-WROOM modules on hand for the real builds — no cost, no lead time.

Why not STM32WB (the original plan)

  • Closed/buggy vendor BLE stack running on a separate M0+ core — source not provided, conflicts with the "write firmware directly against registers" philosophy anyway.
  • Dual-core complexity.
  • Unreliable firmware-upgrade tooling — widely reported issues in ST's own community forums.
  • Too much dev time would be sunk into fighting the platform instead of building the product, on a hard deadline.

Why not STM32 + separate bare radio transceiver chip

  • Would have avoided the STM32WB problems above, using a plain STM32 plus a chip like nRF24L01P or the newer Si24R1.
  • But requires designing/routing a matched antenna — a specialized RF skill outside this project's scope, with high-cost, hard-to-debug failure modes if done wrong.
  • Doesn't sell chip + antenna together the way ESP32 modules do.

Firmware approach

Using Espressif's native ESP-IDF, not the Arduino framework — keeps firmware written directly against the vendor's real low-level SDK, consistent with the project's "no third-party example code" principle (same category as STM32's HAL would have been).

Known tradeoff

ESP-IDF still relies on Espressif's own closed-source Wi-Fi/BT PHY calibration blobs internally — this isn't a full escape from vendor-blob RF stacks, just a major practical improvement over STM32WB's tooling, reliability, availability, and complexity.

Still to confirm during prototyping

  • Realistic minimum BLE connection interval achievable with all 4 controllers connected simultaneously, and how that trades off against battery life
  • Actual BLE behavior in a crowded RF environment (relevant for Open Sauce specifically — lots of other 2.4GHz traffic on the show floor)
  • ESP32-S3 ADC nonlinearity is a known quirk on this chip — shouldn't matter since IMU and buttons are digital (SPI/I2C/GPIO), but worth keeping in mind if any analog input gets added later
