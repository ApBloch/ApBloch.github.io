Battery Selection — Panasonic NCR18650GA (Protected)

Panasonic NCR18650GA Protected Button Top 3450mAh Battery — $10.99/cell, in stock.

Why this cell

  • Protection circuit (over-charge, over-discharge, short-circuit) is built onto the cell itself — no need to design/place a separate DW01+/FS8205-style protection circuit on the PCB. Still need a charge management IC (e.g. MCP73831) for the charging side.
  • Energy-focused cell chemistry — better match for this project's steady ~73mA draw than a high-drain power cell (like the EVE 25P originally considered).
  • 3450mAh capacity — far more than the ~190mAh required (2hr runtime target), plenty of headroom.

Battery Holder — Keystone 1043

Keystone 1043 ended up being the wrong size!!! See link for more info.

Since soldering/welding directly to the cell is prohibited, a PCB-mount holder with spring contacts is the solution — no custom bracket, cushioning, or fabrication needed. Keystone Electronics makes single-cell 18650 holders specifically engineered to withstand the rigors of abuse and recharging cycles associated with 18650 Li-Ion use — a real, distributor-stocked component (DigiKey/Mouser), not a hobbyist compromise.

Note: Keystone 254/54 are NOT full holders — they're bare contact clips only (no plastic body), which would require designing a custom mechanical housing. The real comparison is between 1042 (SMD) and 1043 (through-hole), both complete molded holders.

PartMount typePriceContact resistance
1042 / 1042PSMD~$5.54–8.061.2mΩ
1043 — DECIDEDThrough-hole (PC pin)~$3.02–4.491.2mΩ (identical to 1042)

Why 1043: cheaper than the SMD variant, identical contact resistance (real measured hobbyist data confirms both hit 1.2mΩ), and better mechanical anchoring for a part with constant spring preload plus repeated physical handling during battery swaps — through-hole pins hold up better than SMD pads under that kind of sustained mechanical stress. Being ordered separately from DigiKey rather than in the main reflow SMD batch, so through-hole is also simpler to hand-solder in.

Both variants feature snap-in PC contacts for stability, spring steel/phosphor bronze contacts, and UL 94V-0-rated plastic bodies.

Extra insurance (cheap, zero mechanical effort): 470µF, 10V electrolytic bulk capacitor at the battery input, before the regulator/charge circuit — see the separate Bulk Capacitor Sizing doc for full reasoning. Lets the circuit ride through a few milliseconds of momentary contact interruption without a brown-out/reset, on top of (not instead of) a good holder.

Still to decide

  • Confirm 4.20V vs 4.15V charge target — 4.15V trades some capacity (3450→3360mAh typical) for better long-term cycle life; given 3450mAh is already far more than needed, 4.15V may be the better choice with zero real downside

Decided elsewhere (cross-references)

  • Voltage regulator: TPS63031 (buck-boost) — see MCU/schematic docs
  • Charge IC: MCP73831T-2DCI/OT, 500mA/2kΩ target — see Rough Target Resistors doc
  • Bulk capacitor: 470µF, 10V electrolytic — see Bulk Capacitor Sizing doc
