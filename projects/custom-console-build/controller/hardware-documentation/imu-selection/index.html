Decision

Pick: LSM6DSLTR

LSM6DSLTR is the best option for the final build. The LSM6DSV320X is a high-end/fancy chip with good features, but for this application the LSM6DSLTR is a good middle ground — cheap, readily available, and made by ST so the documentation is solid.

Plan: prototype and do initial testing with the LSM6DSV320X (already owned, used before). Use its wide ±320g range to measure real peak acceleration across a range of throws (soft putt through hard drive). Confirm the LSM6DSLTR's ±16g range isn't clipped before locking it into the final board.

Fallback: LSM6DSO32XTR — same LGA-14L footprint as the LSM6DSLTR, so no layout rework needed if the ±16g range proves insufficient. Note: lower stock availability than the LSM6DSLTR — re-check stock before committing if this becomes the pick.

✅ VALIDATED — Real swing data confirms ±16g (2026-08-11)

Breadboard testing with the LSM6DSV320X captured real peak acceleration across a full range of throws (see Swing Plots and the Breadboard Testing Log, section 2):

Throw typePeak accel (g)
Gentle putt1.85
Medium throw6
Hard drive15
Hardest manageable28 (required an awkward, unnatural jerking stop motion to reach — not representative of a real throw)

Conclusion: ±16g is confirmed the right choice, not just a reasonable guess. A real hard drive peaks around 15g, right at the edge of ±16g but not clipping. Going to ±32g (the LSM6DSO32XTR fallback) would only be trading away resolution across the actual range of real throws to cover an edge case (28g) that only occurs from an unnatural, non-gameplay motion. LSM6DSO32XTR fallback plan is no longer needed — shelving it unless something changes (e.g. a different game mechanic with more violent motion is added later).
