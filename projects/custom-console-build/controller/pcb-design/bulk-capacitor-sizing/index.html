Bulk Capacitor Sizing

Sizing

Core Formula

\(C = \frac{I \cdot \Delta t}{\Delta V}\)

  • I (current draw during the blip): ~73mA at 3.3V after the regulator. On the battery side (before the TPS63031), accounting for ~90% conversion efficiency, roughly ~75-100mA drawn from the battery/cap directly.
  • Δt (blip duration): Using 5ms as a working estimate for a brief mechanical contact interruption.
  • ΔV (allowed droop): TPS63031 operates down to 1.8V input, so there's real headroom below the ~3.6V nominal battery voltage. Allowing 1V of droop before getting uncomfortably close to that floor.

\(C = \frac{0.1A \times 0.005s}{1V} = 500\mu F\)

Lines up with the earlier 100-470µF range mentioned during battery holder research — ~470-500µF is the target.

Capacitor Type

TypeNotes
ElectrolyticCheap, easily hits 470-500µF in a small can, decent ESR. Standard, safe default.
TantalumMore stable, but can fail short under stress — a genuinely bad failure mode sitting directly across a battery. Often avoided for this reason at battery inputs.
Polymer aluminumBetter ESR than standard electrolytic, doesn't share tantalum's short-failure risk. Good upgrade if reasonably priced/sized.
Ceramic (MLCC)Lowest ESR, most robust, but capacitance drops significantly under DC bias — a "470µF" ceramic rated too close to operating voltage may deliver notably less real capacitance. Also large/pricey at this value.

Voltage Rating

Comfortably above 4.2V max charged battery voltage. Standard practice is ~2x margin for electrolytics → 10V-rated part.

Decision

470µF, 10V electrolytic — cheapest, simplest, avoids tantalum's short-failure risk, easily available on JLCPCB.
