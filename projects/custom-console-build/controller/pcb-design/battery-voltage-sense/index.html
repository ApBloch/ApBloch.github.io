Battery Voltage Sense

Battery Voltage Sense — Voltage Divider

Simple rough-gauge battery reading (not a precision fuel gauge). Reads raw battery voltage through a divider into an ADC pin, converted to an approximate charge level in firmware.

Where it taps

Directly off the battery node, before the TPS63031 — the regulator output is a fixed 3.3V regardless of charge level, so reading there would show nothing useful. Needs the raw, sagging battery voltage (2.5V–4.2V range).

Circuit

  • R1 = 100kΩ (battery+ to ADC node)
  • R2 = 100kΩ (ADC node to GND)
  • C1 = 100nF across R2 (ADC node to GND) — gives the ADC a low-impedance source to sample from at the instant of conversion. Standard practice for high-impedance dividers like this (100kΩ source impedance is higher than ideal for a clean instantaneous ADC sample).

1:1 ratio → Vadc = Vbat / 2

Battery stateVbatVadc
Full charge4.2V2.1V
Cutoff2.5V1.25V

Both values sit comfortably clear of the ADC's 0V/3.3V rails — relevant since the ESP32-S3's ADC is known to get less linear near the extremes.

Current draw

~21µA at full charge (4.2V ÷ 200kΩ) — negligible against the ~73mA active budget. Left connected permanently, no switch needed to disconnect between readings.

Firmware note

Li-ion voltage sags very little through the middle of its charge range — a straight-line voltage-to-percentage mapping will be inaccurate in the middle of the curve. Needs a rough lookup table/curve in firmware, not a linear conversion. (Firmware problem, not a hardware one — noted here for later.)
